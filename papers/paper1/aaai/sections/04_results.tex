\section{Controlled Evaluation and Monitor Selection}
\label{sec:results}
\label{sec:pruning}

\paragraph{Controlled detection test.}
A curated corpus of 18 workflows across all four frameworks, seeded with known
anti-patterns, confirms the checks fire when defects are present: the analyzer
detects every injected structural defect and, with a risk-aware sensitive-tool
set, flags 8 workflows whose sensitive path is reachable without a human gate,
versus 10 for the blunt check---declining to flag two that lack a human node but
perform no sensitive action. This is a controlled test that the algorithms work,
not evidence of prevalence. Verification is sub-second on synthetic graphs of
5{,}000 nodes; the supplement compares Promela/SMV modeling effort.

\paragraph{Static monitor selection.}
The graph\,$\times$\,DFA product gives the static machinery a job independent of
whether defects exist: deciding which runtime monitors to deploy. Its value is
\emph{soundness}, not scale, and it rests on the certification gate of
Section~\ref{sec:system}, so every pruning number is sound \emph{relative to an
authored abstraction}---we assume, and do not prove, that a curated workflow's
nodes emit no events outside their declared bindings. A policy whose product is
safe can never fire at runtime, so its monitor need not be deployed;
\texttt{may\_violate} is never suppressed. On the curated corpus the gate
certifies 237 of 270 monitor instances (87.8\%) as provably inert and declines
14 (all on one seeded-livelock workflow) as \texttt{inconclusive}, leaving 19 to
runtime enforcement.

\paragraph{Descriptive vs.\ sound pruning on mined code.}
This rate does not survive lossy extraction. A ${\sim}93\%$ ``pruning'' rate is
available on the mined graphs, but it is pure \emph{alphabet-inertness}: all
13{,}830 mined (workflow, policy) instances are inert only because no mined
workflow invokes any of the 15 policies' tools---a mismatched vocabulary, not a
sound prune. On a may-provenance graph a missing edge could hide exactly the
tool that makes a live monitor look inert, so the gate certifies \emph{none} of
them, refusing all as uncertified extraction.

\paragraph{Does path-sensitivity buy anything?}
Alphabet-inertness needs no product; the product earns its keep only when a
policy's atoms occur and inertness turns on \emph{which} paths reach them. On 18
(workflow, policy) pairs of that kind, a runtime-only strategy certifies 0, since
one unobserved path forbids the claim; alphabet-only prunes 1, node-reachability
3, and the path-sensitive product 10---with no false prunes across 50 concrete
executions. We are deliberately conservative: five of the 9 incremental cases are
linear response-obligation pipelines in which the consequent trivially dominates
every path, and the branching and looping evidence rests on 4 purpose-built
\emph{synthetic} fixtures. Fifty executions cannot establish the absence of
false prunes; this is a mechanism demonstration, not evidence of scale.
