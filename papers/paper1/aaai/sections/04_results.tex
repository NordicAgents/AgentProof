\section{Controlled Evaluation and Monitor Selection}
\label{sec:results}
\label{sec:pruning}

\paragraph{Controlled detection test.}
A curated corpus of 18 workflows spanning all four frameworks, seeded with known
anti-patterns, confirms the checks fire when defects are genuinely present:
Agentproof detects every injected structural defect and, with a risk-aware
sensitive-tool set, flags 8 workflows whose sensitive path (e.g.
\texttt{account\_create}, \texttt{publish}, \texttt{auto\_remediate}) is reachable
without a human gate, versus 10 for the blunt human-presence check---declining to
flag two workflows that lack a human node but perform no sensitive action. This
corpus is a controlled test that the algorithms work, not evidence of prevalence.
Verification is sub-second even for synthetic graphs of 5{,}000 nodes (far larger
than any observed workflow); details, per-framework extractor accuracy on stubs,
and a Promela/SMV modeling-effort comparison are in the supplementary material.

\paragraph{Static monitor selection is an optimization, not a headline.}
The graph\,$\times$\,DFA product gives the static machinery a job that does not
depend on defects existing: deciding which runtime monitors to deploy. Its value
is \emph{soundness}, not scale, and we treat the pruning layer and its policy DSL
as supporting infrastructure for the certification gate of
Section~\ref{sec:system}, which returns \texttt{safe} for a monitor only when the
traversed graph region is certified trace-conservative. A policy whose product is
safe---no resolution of the workflow's declared tool bindings reaches a bad prefix
or a termination point with a pending obligation---can never fire at runtime, so
its monitor need not be deployed; violations (\texttt{may\_violate}) are never
suppressed. On the curated corpus (exact-provenance authored graphs) the gate
certifies 237 of 270 monitor instances (87.8\%) as provably inert and declines 14
(all on one seeded-livelock workflow) as \texttt{inconclusive}, leaving the
remaining 12.2\% (19 that may fire) to runtime enforcement. Returning
\texttt{inconclusive} on a workflow whose cycles could postpone an obligation
forever is an engineering scope choice, not undecidability: universal satisfaction
over finite terminating traces is decidable, and we simply do not bound the
divergent case.

\paragraph{Descriptive vs.\ sound pruning on mined code.}
This certified rate does not survive contact with lossy extraction, and stating so
removes a latent contradiction. What an earlier draft reported as ${\sim}93\%$
``pruning'' on mined graphs is really pure \emph{alphabet-inertness}: all 13{,}950
mined (workflow, policy) instances are alphabet-inert (100\%) because no mined
workflow invokes any of the 15 policies' tools---a descriptive property of a
mismatched vocabulary, not a sound prune. On a may-provenance graph a missing edge
could hide exactly the tool that makes a live monitor look inert. The sound gate
therefore certifies \emph{none} of the 13{,}950 mined instances---every mined
graph carries only \texttt{may}-provenance nodes and edges, so the gate refuses
them all as uncertified extraction---reconciling this section with the paper's
own claim that pruning cannot soundly apply to lossy graphs.

\paragraph{Does path-sensitivity buy anything?}
Alphabet-inertness needs no product; the product earns its keep only when a
policy's atoms actually occur and inertness turns on \emph{which} paths reach them.
We built 18 (workflow, policy) pairs of exactly this kind and compared four
selection strategies (Table~\ref{tab:pruning}). A runtime-only strategy---prune a
monitor if no observed execution triggers it---certifies 0, since one unobserved
path forbids the claim. Alphabet-only prunes 1, node-reachability 3, and the
path-sensitive product 10: 9 more inert monitors than the symbol-set check and 7
more than node reachability, with \emph{no} false prunes across 50 concrete
executions. We are deliberately conservative about what this shows. Five of the 9
incremental cases are linear curated response-obligation pipelines in which the
consequent trivially dominates every path; the genuine evidence of path-sensitivity
on branching and looping topologies rests on the 4 purpose-built \emph{synthetic}
fixtures (labeled synthetic, not sampled)---including a declared-but-unreachable
forbidden tool and an obligation that is bounded across every branch. The
contribution is thus a sound, cheap optimization with a small but real
path-sensitive gain, not a scale result.
